# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Primary:** Students learning financial literacy at their own pace, from foundational concepts through advanced topics. They log in to browse courses, enroll, learn through modules, take quizzes, and discuss with peers in forums.

**Secondary:** Instructors creating and managing course content, monitoring student progress, and engaging in discussions. Admins managing users, courses, and platform health.

**Context:** Learners are seeking to build confidence with finances; they're often new to the topic and may feel overwhelmed by financial concepts. They need a calm, non-judgmental space to learn and ask questions.

## Product Purpose

Cesffo is a financial-literacy learning platform that makes financial education accessible, affordable, and grounded in real-world context. It democratizes financial knowledge by allowing instructors to create courses and students to learn at their own pace—in a community where questions are encouraged and expertise is shared.

Success looks like: students completing courses, gaining confidence in financial concepts, and actively participating in forums without feeling rushed or intimidated.

## Brand Personality

**Japandi:** Japanese + Scandinavian minimalism. Natural, earthy, calm.

**3 words:** Grounded. Warm. Honest.

**Emotional goals:** 
- Learners feel **safe** asking questions about money
- The interface feels **organic** and unhurried—not corporate or sterile
- **Trust** is built through clarity and thoughtful design
- Learning feels like a **meditative practice**, not a chore

**Design direction:** Natural earth tones, warm neutrals, minimalist layout, generous whitespace, and organic shapes. The platform should feel like opening a well-designed journal or stepping into a warm, uncluttered room—the opposite of high-pressure sales or financial anxiety.

## Anti-references

What this should NOT look like:
- Corporate fintech (cold, sleek, black/gold, high-pressure)
- Generic online course platform (templated card grids, notification fatigue)
- Gamified financial "hacks" (urgency, FOMO, quick-win promises)
- Overstimulation (too many gradients, shadows, animations, or decorative elements)
- Inaccessible typography (fancy fonts, low contrast, tiny text)

## Design Principles

1. **Grounded over showy.** Every visual choice serves calm and focus. Warm earth tones and negative space over gradients and complexity.
2. **Honest over polished.** Show real progress and real challenges. Avoid false urgency or manipulative patterns.
3. **Warm over sterile.** Financial education shouldn't feel corporate or intimidating. Natural, approachable, inviting.
4. **Clear hierarchy.** Learners need to find what they're looking for without cognitive overload. One clear path per page.
5. **Inclusive by default.** Keyboard navigable, readable contrast, semantic HTML. Accessibility is not an afterthought.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance as a baseline (good contrast, keyboard navigation, screen reader support). No additional specialized requirements at this stage, but the warm, relaxed aesthetic should not come at the cost of readability or usability.
